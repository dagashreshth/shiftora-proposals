Working the PNR after the sale, by rule

the pattern most relevant to Akbar Travels
One reading, many rulebooks
Musafir.com · UAE · in production

The problem. A leading UAE travel enterprise. Every case arrived as documents in whatever form the customer had them, each checked by hand against rules that differed by case. Errors surfaced at submission, when they cost the most.

What we built. The complete platform in seven weeks, plus the intelligence around it: documents read once with a confidence score, every rulebook held as versioned configuration, and nothing allowed to proceed while it breached one.

Why it worked
  • Read once, applied many times. The same extraction feeds every rule that needs it, rather than being re-keyed for each one.
  • Rules are configuration, not code. Each rulebook changes independently, and the people who own it change it without an engineer.
  • Uncertainty is routed, not guessed. Anything the system is unsure of goes to a person. It never passes silently.

What this means in practice

  • A change request is priced on arrival. Read, computed against that carrier’s rules and drafted before an agent opens it, at any hour.
  • Every figure arrives with its citation. The fare difference, the penalty and the taxes each point at the rule they came from, so approving is checking, not reworking.
  • Refunds stop being chased from memory. Every one is aged against the airline’s own window and escalated while escalating is still cheap.
  • The visa file is read once. Per-country checklists, gaps known on day one, and the applicant asked only for what is genuinely missing.

Why Akbar Travels, and why now

  • The front is already automated. Your portal prices and issues in seconds. The after-sale is where the margin leaks, and it is the half still run by hand.
  • The rules are published and digital. Fare rules are distributed in machine-readable form, and airlines already audit against them with software. This is configuration before judgement.
  • Nothing is replaced. The GDS, the portal and the branches stay exactly as they are. This sits behind them.
  • Your history is the asset. Years of reissues, refunds and debit memos record how every carrier actually behaves. It simply is not searchable yet.

03

What we see at Akbar Travels

Four observations from public sources, each with the business consequence attached.

Observation 01 · the two halves

The front of the business already runs itself.

The portal prices and issues for thousands of sub-agents in seconds, which is real automation, built earlier than most of the industry managed it.

Everything after issuance runs on the opposite model. A reissue, a refund or a schedule change is a person reading fare rules per PNR. The two halves of the same company are decades apart.

Observation 02 · the arithmetic

The margin is thin by design. The touch is expensive by accident.

Consolidation earns a sliver per ticket, priced on the assumption that tickets flow through untouched. A manual touch, one reissue argued over fare rules, costs multiples of that sliver.

One worked PNR can consume the margin of dozens of clean ones. At your volume, the servicing desk decides the profit line, not the sales desk.

Observation 03 · the rulebooks

Every airline is its own fare bible.

Change penalties, refund windows, no-show rules and tax treatments differ by carrier, fare basis and route, and they change without ceremony. Getting one wrong is not a mistake that stays private.

Airlines already enforce these rules with software, and their audit arrives as a debit memo. Today your people answer that software by hand.

Observation 04 · the visa desk

The visa business is the ticket business, repeated.

Sold in minutes, delivered by hand: every application is documents collected, checked against a destination’s requirements and chased to completion, per applicant.

It is also the half where a missed paper does not cost margin. It costs somebody their trip, and you the relationship that sent them.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. Building both the largest network and the portal that feeds it is precisely why the volume exists at all.

The opportunity is that the after-sale is reading requests and applying published rules, the most repeatable work in the company, and the rules are already digital. Answering the airlines’ software with software is not a transformation. It is catching the back of the business up with the front you already built.

Your sub-agent relationships, your airline relationships and your pricing stay untouched.

Where we would start

One place to start. Read every change request on arrival, price it from that carrier’s versioned fare rules, and hand your agents a draft with every figure cited. Nothing changes for sub-agents or airlines. Prove it on one carrier, then widen.

The change desk, drafted by the system
the recommended first build

A request arrives from a sub-agent, a branch or the portal. The system reads the PNR and the ask, applies that carrier’s rules, computes the difference, the penalty and the taxes with every figure cited to its rule, and queues a draft. Refund requests are tracked against the airline’s own window from the moment they open.

Your agents approve. Nothing touches a PNR without a person.

The bigger gain is the debit memos that never arrive. A reissue that cites its rule survives the airline’s audit, and the audit is already a machine.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One carrier, one request type. We map how a reissue is worked today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. Fare rules, refund windows and service-fee policies become versioned settings your team can edit, not code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against the GDS, the portal and the mailboxes your teams already use. No migration. Nothing changes for a sub-agent.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your agents approving every draft.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every ADM and every refund outcome improves the next computation.

How we work

  • One agreed number. Fixed in writing before we start, measured on a date set in advance.
  • Weekly reporting. The same format every week, good news or bad.
  • People decide. The system reads, computes and drafts. Your agents approve everything that touches a PNR.
  • Your environment. Data stays in your systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, updates, retraining and support for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Change how sub-agents reach you. The same portal, the same branches, the same people. This sits behind them.
  • Set your fares or fees. The system applies rules and computes differences. What you charge for a service stays commercial judgement, and stays yours.
  • Send anything automatically. No reissue, no refund and no visa application moves without a person approving it.
  • Expose you to other clients. We work with travel businesses and carry nothing between them, in either direction. Isolation is audited, not assumed.
  • Create dependency. Your team is trained to run it without us.
