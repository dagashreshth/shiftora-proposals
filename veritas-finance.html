What this means in practice

  • The evidence is read at the shop. Khata pages and receipts photographed and read before the officer leaves, so a gap is one more question now rather than a second visit later.
  • The template fills from what was seen. Every figure in the assessment is traceable to a photograph, a page or an observation.
  • Anomalies surface before credit does. Numbers that sit oddly against trade, cluster or history norms are flagged with confidence, for a person.
  • The book is read first. A returning borrower’s history and past assessment pre-fill the renewal instead of starting it blank.

Why Veritas, and why now

  • The craft is the moat, and it walks. Growth opens branches faster than exposure can train assessors, and attrition takes the standard with it. Structure is how a craft stays.
  • The rules are yours already. Credit policy and product norms are written documents. This is configuration work before it is judgement work.
  • Nothing is replaced. The LOS, the branches and the visit itself stay exactly as they are. This sits behind them.

03

What we see at Veritas

Four observations from public sources, each with the business consequence attached.

Observation 01 · the craft

The assessment is authored, not collected.

There is no balance sheet to request. Your officer reconstructs one, from khata books, purchase slips, stock on the shelves and the conversation itself, and writes the income into the file.

Every file is an original composition. The same shop read by two officers can produce two numbers, and credit has to choose which to believe with nothing but the file in front of them.

Observation 02 · the evidence

What the officer saw never fully reaches the file.

The khata page was turned, the stock was counted, the receipts were leafed through, and what survives into the file is a conclusion.

Review trusts the officer because it cannot see what the officer saw. Making the evidence travel with the number is what turns that trust into verification.

Observation 03 · the apprenticeship

The craft takes years, and it walks.

Your best assessors learned by years of shops, corrections and seniors looking over their shoulder. Growth opens branches faster than that exposure can be repeated, and attrition takes it away entirely.

The standard lives in people. Every new branch starts at the bottom of a learning curve the company has already paid for somewhere else.

Observation 04 · the repeat borrower

Your own book is the bureau.

For borrowers who exist nowhere on paper, the bureaus are nearly blank. What does exist is their history with you: every instalment, every delay, every past assessment.

A returning borrower’s file should start half-written from your own record. Today it starts blank, and the reassessment costs what the first assessment did.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. The ability to underwrite people the rest of the system cannot see is the entire moat, and it was built shop by shop.

The opportunity is to structure the capture and the evidence, never the judgement. When what the officer saw travels with the number, review becomes verification, teaching becomes faster, and the craft becomes the company’s instead of the individual’s.

Your credit policy, your field visit and your branch relationships stay untouched.

Where we would start

One place to start. Structure what the field visit captures, read the evidence on the spot, and hand credit an assessment where every number carries its source. Nothing changes in your policy or your visits. Prove it in one region, then widen.

The assessment, with its evidence
the recommended first build

At the shop, the officer photographs what they are already looking at: the khata pages, the receipts, the stock. The system reads it on the spot, fills the assessment template with every figure traceable to its source, flags what sits oddly against trade and cluster norms, and lists what is still missing while the officer can still ask.

Your officers assess. Your credit team sanctions. Nothing is decided by software.

The bigger gain is that the number stops depending on its author. When every figure arrives with what the officer saw, review verifies instead of trusts, and the standard becomes the company’s.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One region. We map how an assessment travels from first visit to sanction and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. Credit policy, product norms and the assessment template become versioned settings your team can edit, not code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against the LOS and the phones your officers already carry. No migration. Nothing changes for a borrower.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your credit team deciding every file.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every sanction, and every default, improves the next assessment.

How we work

  • One agreed number. Fixed in writing before we start, measured on a date set in advance.
  • Weekly reporting. The same format every week, good news or bad.
  • People decide. The system reads, builds and flags. Your officers assess and your credit team sanctions.
  • Your environment. Data stays in your systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, updates, retraining and support for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Change how borrowers reach you. The same branches, the same officers, the same visit to the same shop. This sits behind them.
  • Replace the visit. Nothing here works without your officer standing in the shop. The system structures what the visit captures. It cannot make the visit.
  • Send anything automatically. No sanction, no disbursal and no message to a borrower moves without a person approving it.
  • Expose you to other clients. We work with lending businesses and carry nothing between them, in either direction. Isolation is audited, not assumed.
  • Create dependency. Your team is trained to run it without us.
