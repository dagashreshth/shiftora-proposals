03

What we see at Aye Finance

Four observations from public sources, each with the business consequence attached.

Observation 01 · the asset

Your best data is collected standing up.

A micro-enterprise has no filed accounts and often no bank record worth reading. So you send a person. They see the machines, the stock, the order book and the neighbours, and that visit is the underwriting.

The sharpest credit signal in this market arrives as handwriting, photographs and a form. It decides one loan, and then it stops working.

Observation 02 · the network

Every branch learns its own lessons.

What a good workshop looks like in one trade is learned by the officers who work it. That knowledge is real, it stays local, and it leaves when they do.

So the same trade is assessed slightly differently in every district, and nothing compares the versions to find out which one was right.

Observation 03 · the arithmetic

A small loan carries the same paperwork as a large one.

The visit, the write-up, the verification and the file are broadly fixed per borrower. The ticket is not.

So cost to serve is heaviest exactly where the loans are smallest. That is the difficulty of this market, and the reason so few institutions do it well.

Observation 04 · the officer’s day

The road is the job. The form happens after it.

Travel, assessment and the relationship with a borrower are what a field officer is for. Data entry, document collection and file completion happen around them, usually at the end of the day.

Every hour returned to an officer is an hour spent in front of a borrower, which is the only activity in the business that originates a loan.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. Lending to businesses that no bureau can score is genuinely difficult, and going to look is why it works.

The visit should stay exactly as it is. What happens to it afterwards should not. Reading it, comparing it and filing it are fixed costs on every loan, and they are the reason a small ticket is expensive to serve.

Your visits, your branches and your credit policy stay untouched.

Where we would start

One place to start. Read what the officer captured as it arrives, turn it into the same structured evidence every time, and compare it against what your own book knows about that trade. Nothing changes in the visit itself. Prove it on one cluster, then widen.

Every visit, captured once
the recommended first build

What the officer saw is read as it arrives: the notes, the photographs, the form. It becomes the same structured evidence every time, compared against what your book already knows about that trade and that cluster.

Your credit team decides. Nothing is approved, declined or priced by the system.

The bigger gain is that visits start compounding. What a good workshop looks like stops being one officer’s knowledge and becomes evidence every branch can use.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One cluster, one trade. We map how a visit becomes a decision today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. What each trade should show becomes settings your credit team can edit, held per cluster so one changes without disturbing another.
  • Integration. Read-only against the branch systems and capture your officers already use. No migration. Nothing changes for a borrower.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your credit team deciding on every file.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every loan repaid or missed sharpens what a good business looks like.

How we work

  • One agreed number. Fixed in writing before we start, measured on a date set in advance.
  • Weekly reporting. The same format every week, good news or bad.
  • People decide. The system reads and assembles. Your credit team makes every decision about a loan.
  • Your environment. Data stays in your systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, updates, retraining and support for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Change how you assess a business. The visit stays exactly as it is. Officers keep doing what makes this work.
  • Decide credit. Approving, declining and pricing a loan stay entirely yours.
  • Score a borrower on anything unseen. Every input is something your officer recorded or your borrower produced.
  • Expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited, not assumed. Your borrowers and your book stay yours.
  • Create dependency. Your team is trained to run it without us.
