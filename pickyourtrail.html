What this means in practice

  • Planner hours follow likelihood, not arrival order. The enquiries most likely to travel are worked first, by the people whose time is the actual constraint.
  • The research is done before the case is opened. Availability, sequencing and pricing are assembled in advance rather than gathered while a traveller waits.
  • Nobody is ignored. Lower-scoring enquiries are answered automatically and answered well, rather than answered late by someone who was needed elsewhere.
  • Every enquiry becomes evidence. Booked or lost, from where, at what price, on which destination. Today that history sits in a system as text.

Why Pickyourtrail, and why now

  • The signal already exists. A self-built itinerary is a far richer statement of intent than a form fill. Most businesses that score enquiries would envy the input you already collect.
  • You already hold the labels. Every past enquiry carries its own answer, booked or not. That is exactly what a scoring model is trained on.
  • Nothing is replaced. No change to your itinerary builder, your brand or how travellers reach you. This sits behind them.

03

What we see at Pickyourtrail

Four observations, drawn from public sources and from patterns common to travel businesses with more enquiries than planners. We have seen no internal data, so treat these as hypotheses to correct. Each names the business consequence, not just the symptom.

Observation 01 · the arithmetic

The builder has no gate. Your planners are the gate.

Designing an itinerary online costs a visitor nothing and commits them to nothing. What happens next is a planner working through that same itinerary by hand, whether the person is booking next month or looking at pictures.

So the scarce thing in this business is not enquiries. It is planner hours, and today they are allocated by arrival order rather than by likelihood.

Observation 02 · the handover

Do it yourself still ends with somebody doing it.

A self-built itinerary is a starting point rather than a finished trip. Turning it into something bookable means checking availability, sequencing, transfers, documents and pricing across suppliers, then rebuilding it when one piece moves.

The traveller experiences a product. Your team experiences a research task, repeated once per enquiry, most of which never becomes a booking.

Observation 03 · where your technology points

Your technology is aimed at the plan. Not at the pipeline.

Matching a traveller to flights, stays and activities in minutes is a published capability, and it is the reason the front end feels effortless. That is the right place to have spent the effort first.

What has not been automated is the decision about which of those plans deserves a person, and that single decision sets the cost of every booking you make.

Observation 04 · the window

A holiday is decided over weeks and lost in a day.

An international trip is considered for a long time and then booked quickly, usually when something specific tips it. Through all of that, the same enquiry is being worked by somebody else as well.

So the answer matters most at a moment nobody can predict by watching a queue. Ranking by likelihood is not about ignoring people. It is about being ready when it counts.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. Letting a traveller design their own holiday is a genuinely better product than a form, and it is the reason enquiries arrive at all.

The opportunity is that the product works well enough to produce more real intent than a team of planners can serve, which is a good problem and an expensive one. Nothing here is about answering fewer people. It is about knowing, before an afternoon is spent, which enquiries are a trip and which are a daydream, and answering both properly.

We would not go near your itinerary product, your supplier relationships or your brand. Those are your team’s ground.

Where we would start

One recommendation, not a menu. Read every enquiry together with the itinerary behind it, score the odds it becomes a booked trip, and hand the ones that score highest to a planner with the research already done. No change to your itinerary builder, your brand or how travellers reach you. No platform migration. This exact system already runs in production for a marketplace with the same problem. Prove it on one destination, then let the numbers decide the rest.

Ranked, and already researched
the recommended first build

Every enquiry is read together with the itinerary behind it and scored on the odds it becomes a trip. The ones that score highest arrive with the research already done: availability checked, the sequence tested, pricing assembled and document requirements flagged, so a planner opens a case that is already half answered.

Your planners decide who to call and what to recommend. Nothing is quoted or sent without a person.

Putting the best enquiries first is the obvious gain. It is not the largest one. The same ranking tells you which enquiries never warranted an afternoon, and that is where the capacity for the good ones comes from.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One destination, one channel. We map how an enquiry becomes a booked trip today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. What counts as a strong enquiry, and what a planner sees when one arrives, become settings your team can edit rather than code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against the itinerary builder and the systems your planners already work in. No migration. Nothing changes for a traveller.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your planners calling and recommending as they do now.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every trip booked and every enquiry lost improves the next score.

How we work

  • One number per build, agreed in writing before we start and evaluated on a date fixed in advance.
  • Weekly reporting in a consistent format, to your team, whether the news is good or not.
  • People decide. The system reads, scores and researches. Your planners call, recommend and send.
  • Your environment. Data stays in Pickyourtrail’ systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, rule updates, retraining and support continue for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Not change how travellers reach you. The same builder, the same channels, the same people. This sits behind them.
  • Not decide who is worth talking to. The system ranks and explains why. Which enquiries a planner takes is a commercial judgement and stays yours.
  • Not sell anything automatically. No plan, no price and no commitment reaches a traveller without a planner approving it.
  • Not expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited rather than assumed. Your travellers, your conversion data and your supplier terms stay yours.
  • Not create dependency. Your team is trained to run what we deliver. That it can operate without us is a design requirement.
