What we run in production

A continuous review loop
enterprise logistics operator · in production

The problem. Expert judgement was being applied one case at a time, at national volume. Throughput was capped by how many experienced people were on shift.

What we built. A supervised loop. The system decides and scores its own confidence. Anything uncertain goes to an operator. Every correction becomes training data, so the system improves in production instead of being trusted at launch.

In plain terms

  • One record, not many. A consignment enters once and carries through to filing and invoice. Re-keying between systems disappears, and so do the errors it introduces.
  • Expertise made reusable. The system proposes a classification and shows the past decisions behind it. Your broker still decides, but starts from precedent rather than memory.
  • Errors caught at entry, not at assessment. Values, weights and parties are reconciled before anything is filed, which is the difference between a correction and a query.
  • Revenue captured as it occurs. Charges are recorded at the event rather than reconstructed at month end, so billable work stops being written off.

Why Capricorn, and why now

  • Three divisions, one operating problem. Freight forwarding, brokerage and supply chain all turn on the same decisions, so one layer serves all three.
  • Scale makes consistency valuable. Thirty offices means the same call made by many hands. Standardising it is worth more here than at a single-site operator.
  • It adds to the in-house team. CAPLOG and your developers keep their roadmap. This feeds them cleaner data rather than competing for their time.
  • Volume is the advantage. These systems improve fastest where throughput is highest. Few operators in India are better placed.

03

What we see at Capricorn

Four observations from Capricorn’s published material and filings, and from patterns common to brokerage businesses at this scale. We have seen no internal data, so treat these as hypotheses to correct rather than findings. Each names the business consequence we would expect, not just the operational symptom.

Observation 01 · the growth constraint

Senior expertise is the ceiling.

Classification, valuation and exception calls sit with a limited number of experienced people. Volume can be added quickly; that judgement cannot. The consequence is that growth is priced in senior hires, and a departure takes years of accumulated knowledge with it.

Observation 02 · the network

Thirty offices, thirty interpretations.

More than thirty offices in India and ten abroad. Capability exists in each. What is structurally hard at that spread is the identical case being handled identically in Mumbai, Chennai and Dubai. The consequence is rework, uneven client experience, and a service standard that cannot be evidenced when a large account asks.

Observation 03 · the clock

Delay bills itself.

A query raised at assessment does not only cost handling hours. It consumes free days, and demurrage and detention follow. The consequence is that operational delay lands on a cost line rather than a service metric, and is usually absorbed rather than recovered.

Observation 04 · the build capability

You already build. That is rare.

An in-house development team, the CAPLOG platform, shipment tracking and customer EDI links put Capricorn ahead of most operators its size. What almost never gets built internally is AI that carries a defensible accuracy number, because that is an operating discipline rather than a feature. That is the gap we fill.

Why this matters commercially

None of the above is a failure. It is the standard anatomy of a successful brokerage, and Capricorn’s scale is precisely what makes the arithmetic favourable: on published revenue above ₹500 crore, a single-digit improvement in rework and recovered charges is worth more than most operators earn from a new branch. And we would not go near CAPLOG, the EDI links or your commercial relationships. Those are your team’s ground.

04

Where we would start

One recommendation, not a menu. Start where the three observations intersect: the path a consignment takes from arrival to invoice. It uses data Capricorn already holds, it touches both the clearance clock and the billing line, and the system behind it is already running in logistics. Prove it on one lane, then let the numbers decide the rest.

Arrival to invoice, on one lane
the recommended first build

The system reads what arrives, reconciles it, proposes a classification with the precedent behind it, and assembles the invoice with every charge captured as it occurs. Your broker approves. Nothing files or bills without them, and anything the system is unsure of goes to a person rather than into a declaration.

Handling time is the obvious saving. It is not the largest one. A correction made before filing is a query that never happens, and a query that never happens is free days you never lose.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One branch, one lane. We map how the work is done today and agree the single number the build will be judged against.
  • Design. Your validation rules and client billing terms become configuration your team can edit, not code only we can change.
  • Integration. Read-only against existing data first. CAPLOG stays the system of record and the EDI links are untouched.
  • Deployment. Two weeks in shadow mode beside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than estimated. Then live, with a broker approving every output.
  • Continuous improvement. Accuracy reported weekly to your team. Every correction your brokers make trains the system further.

How we work

  • One number per build, agreed in writing before we start and evaluated on a date fixed in advance.
  • Weekly reporting in a consistent format, to your team, whether the news is good or not.
  • People decide. The system recommends. Your brokers and controllers approve every consequential action.
  • Your environment. Data stays in Capricorn’s systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, rule updates, retraining and support continue for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Not replace CAPLOG, the EDI links or the tracking systems. The manual layer between them is the target.
  • Not file without a broker. The system assembles a declaration. A licensed person submits it. Always.
  • Not reduce headcount for you. We remove repetitive work. What you do with the capacity is your decision.
  • Not expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited rather than assumed. Capricorn’s material stays Capricorn’s.
  • Not create dependency. Your team is trained to run what we deliver. That it can operate without us is a design requirement.
