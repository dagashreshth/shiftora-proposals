03

What we see at Aeronet Worldwide

Four observations, drawn from public sources and from patterns common to time-critical freight operations. We have seen no internal data, so treat these as hypotheses to correct. Each names the business consequence, not just the symptom.

Observation 01 · the quote is the service

By the time they call, they are already in trouble.

Nobody buys expedited freight in a calm moment. A line is down, a part is grounded, a deadline is hours away. The speed of your answer is part of the product.

That makes response time a bigger determinant of win rate than price, and today it depends on who happens to pick up.

Observation 02 · the cost of a miss

A late shipment here is not a service issue.

Customers choose time-critical specifically to avoid a particular outcome. When it happens anyway, the thing they were paying to prevent has occurred.

The cost of a miss is therefore the account, not the fee. And a miss discovered late is a miss that could not be prevented.

Observation 03 · where the judgement lives

Routing expertise sits in individual heads.

Stations across the country and partners abroad each decide routing and carrier from their own experience, under time pressure, one shipment at a time.

The same request therefore gets a different answer depending on who takes it, and that expertise leaves the business when a person does.

Observation 04 · the tools already exist

You built the visibility. Nothing is predicting from it.

A shipping portal, a mobile app, custom integrations and EDI links mean the movements of every shipment are already captured.

The data needed to warn you about a miss is therefore already in the building. What is missing is the thing that reads it and says so in time.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. Attention on every shipment is exactly what customers pay a premium for, and it is why the business has lasted.

The opportunity is that assembling options and watching for slippage are the two most repeatable things your people do under pressure. They are also the two where a machine is genuinely better, because it does not get busy.

We would not go near your pricing judgement, your carrier relationships or your customers. Those are your team’s ground.

04

Where we would start

One recommendation, not a menu. Assemble the quote automatically and predict the miss before it happens, so your stations price and intervene rather than search and discover. No change to how customers reach you. No platform migration. The system behind it already runs in production. Prove it on one station and one service, then let the numbers decide the rest.

Quote assembled, miss predicted
the recommended first build

Urgent requests are read as they arrive, whatever channel they come through. The system assembles routing options across carriers, flights and partners, applies your cost rules, and prepares a quote in your format within minutes.

Once booked, every shipment is scored continuously against its promise. When the odds of a miss rise, your team is told while there is still time to act.

Winning more quotes is the obvious gain. It is not the largest one. A miss you prevent is an account you keep, and in this business that is worth considerably more than the job it was attached to.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One station, one service. We map how a quote is produced today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. Your routing preferences and cost rules become settings your team can edit, not code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against Aerotrac, your EDI links and the carrier data you already receive. No migration. Nothing changes for customers.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your station pricing and sending every quote.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every shipment made or missed sharpens the next prediction.

How we work

  • One number per build, agreed in writing before we start and evaluated on a date fixed in advance.
  • Weekly reporting in a consistent format, to your team, whether the news is good or not.
  • People decide. The system drafts. Your team prices, approves and sends.

  • We stay. Monitoring, rule updates, retraining and support continue for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Not change how customers reach you. The same inbox, the same numbers, the same people. This sits behind them.
  • Not set your prices. The system assembles cost and evidence. Margin is a commercial judgement and stays yours.
  • Not send anything automatically. No quote and no commitment reaches a customer without a person approving it.
  • Not expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited rather than assumed. Your rates and customers stay yours.
  • Not create dependency. Your team is trained to run what we deliver. That it can operate without us is a design requirement.
