03

What we see at Ador Welding

Four observations from public sources, each with the business consequence attached.

Observation 01 · the front door

Every enquiry starts as a paragraph.

The only technical field on your enquiry form is a free-text box headed “Tell us about your requirement.” No base metal, no thickness, and no product selector anywhere on the site.

So every enquiry is read by an engineer before it can be answered. The queue starts before the work does.

Observation 02 · the catalogue

The catalogue is deeper than any one memory.

The range spans carbon steel, low alloy, stainless, cellulosic and hardfacing, each grade carrying its own AWS classification (the code that fixes what a consumable is certified to do).

Matching a description to the right grade is expert judgement, repeated from scratch on every enquiry.

Observation 03 · the network

The distributor carries the product, not the answer.

Distributors put the product within reach of the whole country. The technical question still travels back to your engineers.

Reach scales with distributors. Answering scales only with headcount.

Observation 04 · the cost curve

Growth is being bought with engineers.

While reading and matching stay manual, every increase in volume needs another application engineer.

Margin per enquiry then stays flat as you grow. Scale is supposed to do the opposite.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. It is how almost every technical manufacturer is built.

Reading an enquiry and matching it to a grade is the most repeatable task here, which makes it the cheapest to change and the fastest to prove.

We would not touch the welding recommendation or your distributor relationships. That is your team’s ground.

Where we would start

One place to start. Read every enquiry and draft the grade and price for an engineer to approve. Prove it on one product line, then widen.

Enquiry to draft recommendation
the recommended first build

The system reads each enquiry on arrival, extracts base metal, thickness, position and standard, and matches them against your own catalogue and welding procedures.

It drafts the recommendation and the price. Your engineer approves or changes it. Nothing reaches a customer unapproved.

The faster answer is not the largest gain. Every recommendation becomes evidence: what was recommended, for which job, and whether it won.
