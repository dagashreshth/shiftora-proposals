What we run in production

Two systems, both running today. One ranks a large pool by expected value and explains every rank. The other is what makes automated decisions safe to trust at volume. We show these before proposing anything.

Ranking a pool nobody can work evenly

the pattern most relevant to Fuze
Calibrated ranking, in production
LegalMatch.com · USA · in production

The problem. A US marketplace approaching $100M revenue with far more inbound demand than its sales floor could work. Effort was spread evenly across every enquiry, so the best opportunities got the same attention as the worst and nobody could defend the order of work.

What we built. Three calibrated probability models, one per funnel stage, inside the system the team already used. Every opportunity ranked by expected value, and every ranking explainable to the person acting on it.

Why it worked
  • Calibrated, not just ordered. When the model says 60 percent, roughly 60 in 100 happen. That makes the score usable for planning, not only for sorting.
  • Explainable at the row level. The person sees why something ranks where it does. Ranking without reasons gets ignored however accurate it is.
  • Corrections feed back. Every human override becomes training data, so the model tracks the market rather than the month it was built.

  • Your history is the asset. Every load you have ever covered is a labelled example. They simply are not being learned from.

03

What we see at Fuze

Four observations, drawn from public sources and from patterns common to fast-growing brokerages. We have seen no internal data, so treat these as hypotheses to correct. Each names the business consequence, not just the symptom.

Observation 01 · the unused asset

A network that large cannot be worked from memory.

Assembling a carrier base of this depth takes years and is genuinely hard to replicate. It is the reason a shipper calls you rather than a smaller broker.

But a broker under time pressure calls the carriers they know. Most of the network is invisible on any given load, which means capacity you already have is bought from someone else or not bought at all.

Observation 02 · where margin is decided

The spread is set in minutes, from memory.

Brokerage margin is the gap between what a shipper is quoted and what a carrier accepts, and both numbers are committed within minutes of each other under pressure.

Quote high and lose the load. Quote low and cover it at a loss. That judgement is made hundreds of times a week on recall rather than on what the lane has actually cleared at.

Observation 03 · growth by acquisition

Two networks only help if something sees both.

Extending reach by acquisition adds carriers, lanes and relationships quickly, which is the fastest way to grow a brokerage.

It also doubles the memory problem. Without a shared view, a broker in one office cannot use capacity the other office spent years earning.

Observation 04 · the standard you set

Calling yourself next-generation raises the bar.

Positioning on technology is the right call in a market where most brokerages still compete on relationships alone, and it is presumably already winning accounts.

It also means what you deploy has to carry a number you can show a shipper, not a demonstration. That is an operating discipline rather than a feature.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. Every brokerage in the market works this way, because relationships genuinely are the business and good brokers genuinely do know their lanes.

The opportunity is that carrier selection and pricing are the two highest-frequency decisions in the building, made under the most time pressure, with the most data already sitting behind them. That combination is rare and it is why the return here is fast.

We would not go near your carrier relationships, your commercial terms or who your brokers choose to call. Those are your team’s ground.

Where we would start

One recommendation, not a menu. Rank the carriers who actually fit each load and propose a price from what the lane has cleared at, so brokers choose from evidence rather than recall. No change to your systems. No change to who your brokers call. The system behind it already runs in production. Prove it on one lane group, then let the numbers decide the rest.

Carrier ranking and price guidance
the recommended first build

Each load is read as it arrives. The system ranks carriers on likelihood to accept, likelihood to deliver on time and expected rate, shows one line of reasoning behind each, and proposes buy and sell prices from what that lane has actually cleared at.

Your broker decides who to call and what to charge. Nothing tenders itself and no rate goes to a shipper without a person.

Faster coverage is the obvious gain. It is not the largest one. A ranked list surfaces carriers your team has never used but who fit the lane, which turns a network you already pay to maintain into capacity you can actually reach.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One lane group, one desk. We map how coverage and pricing are decided today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. Your margin floors and carrier requirements become settings your team can edit, not code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against your load history and carrier records. No migration. Nothing changes for shippers or carriers.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your brokers choosing and pricing every load.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every acceptance, refusal and late delivery sharpens the next ranking.

How we work

  • One number per build, agreed in writing before we start and evaluated on a date fixed in advance.

What we will not do

  • Not change your carrier relationships. Who your brokers call and what they agree stays entirely theirs.
