What this means in practice

03

What we see at Jeena

Four observations from public sources, each with the business consequence attached.

Observation 01 · the asset

The firm knows more than any system holds.

A century of forwarding leaves a real body of knowledge: which route works in which season, which handler to trust, what a shipment like this has cost before. It is why a shipper calls Jeena rather than a portal.

All of it sits with people. It is the most valuable thing the business owns and the only asset that is not written down anywhere.

Observation 02 · the reply

Every quote begins with an empty page.

An enquiry arrives, and somebody starts working out what it should cost. Not because the answer is unknown, but because the last time this route was quoted is in a mailbox rather than in a place anyone can search.

In freight the first credible number very often wins the shipment. Response speed is therefore a revenue number wearing an operations costume.

Observation 03 · the branches

The same enquiry, priced from different memories.

A branch network means the identical request is worked separately in each place, from what the people there happen to know.

That produces different numbers on comparable routes, no shared view of demand, and a firm that cannot see its own pricing.

Observation 04 · the handover

Knowledge leaves with the person who has it.

A business run by successive generations has managed this before, which is precisely why it is worth saying plainly. Every retirement takes routes, relationships and judgement with it.

Written down, that knowledge becomes the firm’s. Left in memory, it has to be rebuilt by whoever comes next, one enquiry at a time.

Why this matters commercially

None of this is a failure. It is how every long-lived forwarder is built, because the people really are good at the work.

The opportunity is that the firm has a century of answers and no way to look them up. Writing that down does not replace anyone’s judgement. It gives the next person the same starting point the last one had.

Your pricing judgement, your carrier relationships and your customers stay untouched.

Where we would start

One place to start. Read every enquiry on arrival and answer it with a draft priced from what the route has actually shipped at. Nothing changes in how shippers reach you or how your branches work. Prove it on one trade lane, then widen.

Quoted from what you already know
the recommended first build

An enquiry is read on arrival, in whatever channel it came in. The system pulls what that route has actually shipped at, applies your costing and handling rules, and puts a draft in front of the person who would have written it.

Your team sets the price and sends it. Nothing is quoted on the system’s word.

The bigger gain is that the firm starts remembering. Every quote becomes a record instead of a sent email, so what the business knows stops depending on who is still at the desk.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One branch, one trade lane. We map how a quote is produced today and agree the single number we will be judged on.
  • Design. Your costing, handling and margin rules become versioned settings your own people can edit, not code only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against the mailboxes and rate sources your branches already use. No migration. Nothing changes for a shipper.
  • Deployment. Two weeks running quietly alongside the live process, so the baseline is measured rather than guessed. Then live, with your team pricing every quote.
  • Continuous improvement. Results reported weekly. Every quote won or lost, on every route, improves the next one.

How we work

  • One agreed number. Fixed in writing before we start, measured on a date set in advance.
  • Weekly reporting. The same format every week, good news or bad.
  • People decide. The system drafts. Your team prices, approves and sends.
  • Your environment. Data stays in your systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, updates, retraining and support for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Change how shippers reach you. The same inboxes, the same numbers, the same people at the branch. This sits behind them.
  • Set your prices. The system assembles cost and evidence. Margin is a commercial judgement and stays yours.
  • Send anything automatically. No quote and no commitment reaches a shipper without a person approving it.
  • Expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited, not assumed. Your rates, your carriers and your customers stay yours.
  • Create dependency. Your team is trained to run it without us.
