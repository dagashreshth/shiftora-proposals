01

What we run in production

Two systems matter for this conversation. One ranks accounts by the probability they convert, live in a US marketplace. The other is a supervised loop that makes AI decisions safe to trust at volume. Cogoport needs both, and they are shown here as they run before anything is proposed.

Scoring demand

the pattern most relevant to Cogoport
Ranking a funnel nobody could work evenly
US marketplace · ~$100M revenue · in production

The problem. Far more inbound demand than a hundred-strong sales floor could handle. Effort was spread evenly across every enquiry, so the best opportunities got the same attention as the worst, and nobody could defend the order of work.

What we built. Three calibrated probability models, one per funnel stage, inside the CRM the team already used. Every account ranked by expected value, and every ranking explainable to the rep working it, which is what made them trust it.

Why it worked
  • Calibrated, not just ordered. When the model says 60 percent, roughly 60 in 100 convert. That makes the score usable for forecasting and staffing, not only for sorting.
  • Explainable at the row level. A rep sees why an account ranks where it does. Ranking without reasons gets ignored, however accurate it is.
  • Corrections feed back. Every human override becomes training data, so the model tracks the market rather than the month it was built.

Making it safe to trust

the discipline behind every system we ship
A continuous review loop
enterprise logistics operator · in production

The problem. Expert judgement applied one case at a time, at national volume. Throughput capped by how many experienced people were on shift.

What we built. A supervised loop. The system decides and scores its own confidence. Anything uncertain goes to a person. Every correction becomes training data, so accuracy improves in production instead of being trusted at launch.

Why it holds up
  • Nothing silent. Accuracy is reported weekly against live traffic. A figure that stops passing its test stops being claimed.
  • Nothing unearned. Autonomy is granted queue by queue, only as the numbers hold.
  • Nothing unattributable. Every decision carries its reasoning, confidence and audit trail.

In plain terms

  • Intent becomes visible before it books. A search on a lane, twice in a week, is evidence. Today it looks the same as every other search.
  • The queue orders itself. Your team opens a ranked list each morning instead of deciding where to start.
  • Every rank comes with a reason. One line explaining why an account is near the top, which is the difference between a tool that gets used and one that gets closed.
  • Support answers from your own material. Rate rules, schedules and policies, cited, so a wrong answer is traceable and correctable rather than invisible.

Why Cogoport, and why now

  • The signal already exists. Self-serve generates intent data that traditional forwarders never see. It is being logged. It is not being scored.
  • Breadth multiplies the opportunity. FCL, LCL, air, customs, domestic, insurance and financing means every account has a next product. Nobody has time to work out which.
  • You already ship AI. CogoAI exists, so the argument for AI is settled internally. What we add is the measurement discipline that makes it safe to widen.
  • Volume is the advantage. Scoring models improve fastest where behavioural data is richest, and a platform at your scale is close to the best case.

03

What we see at Cogoport

Four observations from what Cogoport publishes, and from patterns common to self-serve marketplaces. We have seen no internal data, so treat these as hypotheses to correct rather than findings. Each names the business consequence we would expect, not just the operational symptom.

Observation 01 · the unscored funnel

Every search is a signal. Few are treated as one.

Cogoport publishes instant quoting across 11,075 port pairs for 30,000+ importers and exporters. That produces intent data a traditional forwarder never sees. The consequence is that commercial effort is allocated by whoever shouts loudest or fills in a form, while the strongest buying signals sit unread in a log.

Observation 02 · the service equation

Support scales with users, not with bookings.

A self-serve base generates questions whether or not it converts. Rate validity, documentation, schedules, tracking and payment queries arrive from everyone. The consequence is a cost line that grows with registrations rather than revenue, which is the structural pressure on every marketplace margin.

Observation 03 · breadth

More products, more unasked questions.

FCL, LCL, air, customs, CFS, domestic road and rail, insurance, Pay Later, export factoring and FX. Each is a reason for an existing customer to return. The consequence is that cross-sell depends on someone noticing the right moment, which does not scale and is invisible when it fails.

Observation 04 · the AI already shipped

CogoAI settles the argument. It also raises the bar.

An assistant is already in the product, so nobody internally needs convincing that AI belongs here. The consequence is that the next question is no longer whether to use AI but whether you can prove what it gets right, and that is an operating discipline rather than a feature. It is what we bring.

Why this matters commercially

None of the above is a failure. It is the standard anatomy of a platform that has won distribution and is now working out how to monetise attention. On a marketplace, a single-digit shift in which accounts get worked is worth more than any amount of additional traffic, because the traffic is already there. And we would not go near your pricing, your carrier relationships or the platform itself. Those are your team’s ground.

04

Where we would start

One recommendation, not a menu. Score the demand you already capture, so the commercial team works a ranked list instead of a flat one. It uses data Cogoport already logs, it needs no change to the customer experience, and the system behind it is running in a US marketplace today. Prove it on one segment, then let the numbers decide the rest.

Demand scoring, on one segment
the recommended first build

The system reads the behaviour you already log, scores each account on the probability it ships in the next window, ranks the day’s work by expected value, and gives one line of reasoning behind every rank. Your team decides who is contacted and what is offered. Nothing is sent automatically and nothing changes in the customer-facing product.

The obvious gain is conversion. It is not the largest one. A ranked queue tells you which accounts are going quiet while they are still reachable, and that is retention, which compounds where new acquisition does not.

How the rollout runs
  • Discovery. One segment, one region. We map how the commercial team works today and agree the single number the build will be judged against.
  • Design. The features and thresholds become configuration your team can inspect and change, not a model only we understand.
  • Integration. Read-only against existing behavioural and booking data. The platform and CogoAI are untouched.
  • Deployment. Two weeks scoring in shadow, so the baseline is measured rather than estimated. Then live, with the team free to ignore any rank they disagree with.
  • Continuous improvement. Calibration and accuracy reported weekly. Every outcome, won or lost, trains the next version.

How we work

  • One number per build, agreed in writing before we start and evaluated on a date fixed in advance.
  • Weekly reporting in a consistent format, to your team, whether the news is good or not.
  • People decide. The system ranks and recommends. Your team chooses who is contacted and what is said.
  • Your environment. Data stays in Cogoport’s systems and jurisdiction. We integrate rather than extract.
  • We stay. Monitoring, retraining and support continue for the life of the system.

What we will not do

  • Not touch the customer experience. No change to search, quoting or booking. This runs behind the platform.
  • Not replace CogoAI or your team’s roadmap. We would rather measure what you have built than rebuild it.
  • Not send anything automatically. The system proposes who to contact. A person decides and a person writes.
  • Not expose you to other clients. Isolation is audited rather than assumed. Cogoport’s data stays Cogoport’s.
  • Not create dependency. Your team is trained to run what we deliver. That it can operate without us is a design requirement.
